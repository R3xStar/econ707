2  Course Information

2.1 Course Description

The objective of this course is to introduce students to time series analysis and forecasting. The goal is for students to obtain hands-on experience using financial, economic, business, crime, traffic, and other data to construct and evaluate forecasts. The course introduces several techniques for applied time series analysis and forecasting. Students will use R to enhance their computer skills.

This is an accelerated eight-week section covering the same material as the standard sixteen-week course.

Prerequisite: ECON 706 or equivalent.

2.2 Course Objectives

At the end of this course, students will be able to:

  1. Understand and apply common statistical methods used in business and economic forecasting
  2. Explain, identify, and extract components of a time series
  3. Collect, organize, visualize, and analyze time series data
  4. Interpret and critique forecasts

2.3 Course Format

There are no live class meetings. The course is organized into eight Canvas modules, one per week. Each module opens at the start of its week and contains the assigned reading, recorded lecture videos, annotated R scripts, and the graded work for that week.

Unless stated otherwise, all module work is due at 11:59 pm ET on the Sunday that closes the module week. Modules remain open for review after their due dates, so you may work ahead, but you may not fall behind.

2.4 Required Texts

None. All required readings are freely available online and linked in Canvas.

2.7 Software

As this course focuses on the learning and application of time series techniques, a computer or laptop with R and R Studio installed is required for module work, assignments, and exams. You will also install a number of open-source packages that facilitate economic analysis in R.

You may use Posit Cloud instead of a locally installed version of R.

You must be able to render R Markdown or Quarto documents to PDF or HTML for submission. Technology problems are not grounds for an extension in an eight-week course — submit early.

2.9 Forecasting Project

The goal is for everyone to design an individual project, make decisions, and have some fun. You will choose a specific time series, make a set of forecasts, and evaluate your success. You will work on this project throughout the session, submitting a pitch, a data report, and a final project report.

Your series must have at least one new realization between when you submit your forecast and when you submit your evaluation. Because the session is only eight weeks long, series selection matters more than it does in a full semester: your pitch must identify the release date of the realization you intend to forecast, and that date must fall before the end of Week 7. Series whose next release falls outside the session will not be approved.

2.10 Exams

There are two exams. The midterm covers Modules 1–3. The final is comprehensive and covers Modules 1–7. Both are administered in Canvas as timed, single-attempt exams during a defined window, and both mix conceptual, theoretical, and interpretive questions. You may use R and your own notes; you may not consult another person or an AI system.

2.11 Homework

There are five homework assignments, submitted through Canvas. Each requires you to produce and interpret output in R. Submit both your write-up and your code.

2.12 Participation

In an asynchronous course, participation means completing each module’s activities within its week. Canvas records module progress and submission times, and these records document participation. Students who fail to submit graded work for more than one module week are not making satisfactory progress and are subject to failure. If circumstances put you behind, contact me immediately rather than after the fact.

2.13 Academic Integrity

Academic dishonesty will not be tolerated. This includes unauthorized use of artificial intelligence tools. Please use AI as a resource for learning and not as a substitute for your submission. AI use is not permitted on either exam. All cases of academic dishonesty are reported to the Office of Student Conduct and Academic Integrity.

2.14 Educational Accessibility

Old Dominion University is committed to ensuring equal access to all qualified students with disabilities. If you experience a disability that will impact your ability to access any aspect of this class, please present me with an accommodation letter from the Office of Educational Accessibility. Because the session is compressed, please do this in Week 1 so accommodations can be in place before the midterm.

The Office of Educational Accessibility is located at 1021 Student Success Center, 757-683-4655.
